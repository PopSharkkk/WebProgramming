Hometown

Surabaya

The provincial capital of East Java and Indonesia's second largest metropolitan region.

City Profile

Port City, Economic Center, and Historic Ground

Surabaya serves as the primary trade gateway and commercial center for eastern Indonesia. Situated on the mouth of the Kalimas River along the Madura Strait, the city has maintained its strategic port importance for over seven centuries.

The populace comprises multi-ethnic communities including Javanese, Madurese, Chinese-Indonesian, and Arab descents. The municipal culture is characterized by straightforward communication and active community solidarity, historically reflected during the national revolution of November 1945.

Chronology

Historical Evolution

1293

Establishment & Majapahit Victory

The settlement mark commemorates Raden Wijaya's repulsion of the Mongol Yuan fleet on May 31, 1293, officially celebrated as the municipal founding date.

17th — Early 20th Century

Colonial Maritime Port

Developed under the VOC and Dutch colonial administration into the primary naval base and export terminus for sugar, coffee, and agricultural produce from East Java.

November 10, 1945

The Battle of Surabaya

A defining engagement of the Indonesian National Revolution, in which local militias resisted British and Indian forces, solidifying international diplomatic recognition for Indonesian sovereignty.

Present Era

Contemporary Urbanization

Modern Surabaya functions as an industrial and academic hub, notable for municipal environmental administration, public pedestrian spaces, and the Tanjung Perak shipping terminal.
